Simulation Parameters

Model: Logistic Sigmoid
Carrying Capacity ($L$) 2500 g

Max limit of fresh larval biomass supported by the standard 1kg food-waste substrate tray.

Growth Rate Constant ($k$) 0.45

Impact of metabolic speed. Higher values denote ideal thermal & humidity controls ($30^\circ\text{C}$, $70\%$ RH).

Inflection Point ($t_0$) Day 10

The day when growth transitions from acceleration to deceleration. Theoretically the mathematically perfect harvest day.

The Applied Mathematics

Cumulative Biomass Yield Curve:

$$f(t) = \frac{L}{1 + e^{-k(t - t_0)}}$$

First Derivative (Growth Velocity):

$$f'(t) = \frac{L \cdot k \cdot e^{-k(t - t_0)}}{\left(1 + e^{-k(t - t_0)}\right)^2}$$

Derived using Chain and Quotient rules. This outputs the instantaneous biological yield accumulation ($g/\text{day}$).

Tangent Line Formula at Day $c$:

$$y = f'(c) \cdot (t - c) + f(c)$$

The derivative $f'(c)$ represents the precise geometric slope of the tangent line touching the biomass curve at that specific millisecond.

Agronomic State Evaluation
Starting Inoculation

① Lag Phase (Days 0 - 5)

Larvae adjust to feed substrate in this initial period. The derivative value $f'(t)$ is low ($< 50\text{ g/day}$), representing slow weight accretion as cellular setup dominates.

② Inflection Phase (Days 8 - 12)

The peak metabolic surge. Larvae consume vast feed volumes. The derivative curve hits its global absolute maximum value precisely at Day $t_0$. Optimal nutritional conversion metrics occur here.

③ Deceleration & Prepupae (Days 15 - 20)

Biomass stabilizes as larvae cease feeding and prepare for pupation. The derivative $f'(t)$ drops back towards zero, indicating that holding larvae longer yields diminishing returns.
